1Welcome: what you are getting

Your store runs on three connected pieces:

Active Paw storefrontYour Shopify theme: the shop customers see, installable on their phones like an app.
PawScoutThe operator: answers customer email, syncs stock and prices, computes delivery promises, runs marketing. Lives as an app inside your Shopify admin.
Your supplierCJDropshipping: source of products, stock levels, warehouses and delivery routes.

PawScout's job is to make a one-person store operate like a staffed one. Customer email gets answered with drafts you approve (or, when you allow it, automatically). Stock, prices and delivery estimates refresh themselves nightly from supplier truth. Marketing is built so that following the law is the default, not an effort.

One principle runs through everything and is worth knowing from day one: the platform only tells customers things it can verify. If a delivery time, a stock level or a business fact cannot be grounded in live data, the customer sees a safe, honest hedge instead. This protects your reputation and keeps you on the right side of consumer law without you having to police it.

3Installation and onboarding

Onboarding is white-glove: the PawScout team provisions the platform pieces and you approve each connection to your property. The sequence is:

  1. Install the PawScout app. You receive an install link for your store. Shopify shows the permissions the app needs (products, inventory, orders, draft orders, publications, shipping, markets and customer read access among them); approving completes the connection. No API keys change hands: Shopify's own managed-install token exchange authenticates the app.
  2. Install the Active Paw theme. The theme is pushed to your store as an unpublished theme first, so you can preview it against your real catalog before publishing. Brand name, logo, favicon and colours are set in Shopify's Theme Editor, no code involved.
  3. Wire your email domain. We provision sending and receiving on your domain (a handful of DNS records: verification, DKIM, routing). After this, support mail to your domain flows into the PawScout Inbox, and outbound mail is authenticated so it lands in inboxes rather than spam folders.
  4. Connect the supplier. In the console's Settings view, paste your CJDropshipping API key into Connect CJ. The key is sealed into the encrypted vault and verified with a live call.
  5. Seed the catalog. From the Catalog view, choose categories and import an initial product set. Imports arrive as drafts with AI-curated titles, descriptions and images for your review before publishing.
  6. Let the first nightly run complete. Overnight, the platform stamps stock, prices, delivery estimates and shipping profiles for everything imported. From then on it maintains itself.

4First-day setup, step by step

Everything below happens in the PawScout console (Apps > PawScout inside your Shopify admin), mostly in the Settings view.

4.1 Connect CJ

Paste the API key from CJ's developer settings. The status line confirms the connection and the key disappears into the vault; it is never shown again.

4.2 Choose your AI models

The AI models card selects which model powers replies and which vision-capable model screens product images. The defaults are sensible; you can change them at any time without breaking anything.

4.3 Set the reply mode

The Reply mode and per-agent auto-send card is the most important switch in the product. Start in review mode: every customer email gets a drafted reply that waits for your one-click approval. As you watch categories perform, flip individual agents (order status is usually first) to auto-send. You stay in review mode for anything you have not explicitly automated.

4.4 Inbox settings

Set your alert email (urgent notifications, campaign tests) and your business postal address. The postal address appears in the small print of marketing email and is a legal requirement; campaigns will refuse to send until it is saved. Use a virtual office address if you do not want to publish your home address.

4.5 Storefront checks

  • Open your store on a phone: you should see the install prompt offering to add the store to the home screen.
  • Add a product to the cart: the drawer should show delivery facts and one-tap add-on suggestions.
  • Check the footer: legal pages (privacy, returns, terms) are linked automatically from your Shopify policies.

4.6 Run the policy keeper

In Campaigns > Privacy policy wording, click the check. If your privacy policy is missing the marketing-email disclosures (opt-in basis, engagement measurement, unsubscribe, business address), one click applies the recommended wording. Requires Shopify's automatic policy management to be off, so the text is yours to edit.

4.7 Turn on email analytics

In Campaigns > Email analytics, click Turn on tracking once. From then on every email reports delivery, opens and clicks, and bad addresses are suppressed automatically.

6Running the store day to day

What happens without you

  • Every night: stock levels sync from the supplier into Shopify, prices re-check against cost rules, delivery estimates restamp from the live logistics matrix, shipping profiles and market availability reconcile, and thin collections refill from the import pipeline.
  • Every 15 minutes: new orders move through the pipeline, new email is classified and drafted, and any in-flight nightly work resumes.
  • Continuously: email engagement events flow in; hard bounces and complaints go straight to the suppression list.

What needs you

  • The attention queue on Glance, and draft approvals in the Inbox. In review mode this is minutes per day: read, approve, occasionally edit.
  • Campaign sends. Nothing broadcasts without your click, ever.
  • Partner decisions. Approving applications and flipping marketing consent when a partner opts in.
  • Catalog taste. Reviewing imported drafts before they publish keeps the shop's voice yours.
A good weekly rhythm: Monday, read Glance and the Audit view; midweek, review campaign metrics and partner performance; Friday, update the Traffic board's next actions from what the week's numbers said.

7Marketing: campaigns, partners and the welcome offer

Campaigns

  1. Compose in plain text, pick the audience: buyers (customers who ticked the marketing opt-in at checkout, read live from Shopify at send time) or partners (collaborators with a recorded opt-in).
  2. Send yourself a test. It is the exact production email, unsubscribe link and all, with only the subject marked as a test.
  3. Send, and confirm the second click. Sending proceeds in small batches with live progress.
  4. While sending you can pause (instant, reversible; nobody is emailed twice on resume) or stop (final; the record of who received it stays). Drafts can be edited or discarded; finished campaigns duplicate into fresh drafts.
  5. Watch delivered / opened / clicked on the campaign row once tracking is on. Clicks are the reliable signal; treat opens as a trend line.

Partners

Applications arrive as inbox threads and appear in the Partners view. Approving one issues a tracked voucher code; orders using it attribute to the partner automatically. Revoking a voucher deactivates its code immediately. Partners only join the campaigns audience after they have explicitly said yes to marketing, which the invitation asks them directly.

The welcome offer

The storefront's welcome modal offers a discount event with a real end date, obeys interstitial etiquette (never instantly on load, never twice), and applies the code for the customer so nobody fails to redeem it. Voucher issue and trial entries are automated behind deterministic gates.

8Your compliance responsibilities

The platform makes the mechanical parts of email and consumer law automatic, but three things remain yours:

  • Register with the ICO (UK stores). As a business processing personal data you are expected to be registered with the Information Commissioner's Office; most small businesses pay a modest yearly fee.
  • Keep your policies truthful. The policy keeper maintains the marketing disclosures, but returns windows, delivery promises and contact details in your policies should match how you actually operate. The defaults we provision do.
  • Use a publishable postal address. The address in marketing footers and policies is public. A virtual office address is fine; decide deliberately.
What the platform will refuse to do for you: email people who have not opted in, hide the unsubscribe link, send marketing without a postal address, claim stock or delivery times it cannot verify, or state a dispatch origin it cannot prove. These are not settings; they are how it is built.

9Data, security and what we store

  • Credentials (your supplier API key, tokens, webhook secrets) are sealed in an encrypted vault: a per-tenant data key, itself wrapped by a platform master key. They are never logged and never shown back to you after entry.
  • Customer data is minimised. Marketing consent is read live from Shopify rather than copied, so an opt-out is effective immediately. The platform keeps conversation threads, an anonymised send ledger (which address received which campaign), engagement events, and the suppression list.
  • Isolation: every record is scoped to your shop. Tenants cannot see each other's data.
  • Infrastructure: managed edge hosting (Cloudflare Workers and D1) with scheduled jobs; no servers of yours, nothing to patch.
  • Email authentication: your domain sends with proper verification (DKIM), which is both deliverability and anti-spoofing protection.

10Troubleshooting and common questions

SymptomWhat to check
Campaign will not sendAlmost always the missing business postal address (Settings > Inbox settings, or the Sender details card in Campaigns). The error message says exactly what is missing.
A reply draft looks wrongClick regenerate on the thread; if the category keeps misfiring, leave that agent in review mode and tell us. Never edit-and-send something you would not say yourself.
Product shows "unavailable" unexpectedlyThe supplier stock fell under the safety floor and the platform paused it. It recovers automatically when the supplier restocks; the Audit view shows the pause event.
Delivery estimate seems conservativeEstimates are processing plus door-to-door transit plus a small buffer, from live supplier data. A product not yet stamped shows a safe fallback until its first nightly sync.
Opens look lowSome mail apps block open tracking; clicks are the trustworthy number. Judge campaigns on clicks and orders.
The console looks staleReload the tab; the console is a live page inside Shopify admin. Job health stamps on Glance show when each scheduled task last completed.

Support: your onboarding contact remains your support channel. Include the shop domain and, where relevant, the campaign or thread you are looking at; the Audit view timestamp helps us find anything fast.

11Glossary

AgentA specialised AI responder for one category of customer email.
Review mode / HITLHuman-in-the-loop: drafts wait for your approval instead of sending automatically.
Draft (campaign)A saved broadcast that has not been sent; the only campaign state that can be edited.
Suppression listAddresses that must never receive marketing again: unsubscribes, hard bounces, spam complaints.
Send ledgerThe per-recipient record that makes campaign sending resumable and duplicate-proof.
Stock floorThe supplier stock level below which a product is automatically paused for safety.
Delivery bandThe order-to-door day range shown to customers, such as "3-10 days", computed from live logistics data.
Nearest-warehouse ruleEvery delivery time and cost is calculated from the supplier warehouse closest to the customer that actually stocks the item.
Attention queueThe Glance list of items that need a human decision.
VaultThe encrypted per-tenant store for API keys and secrets.
